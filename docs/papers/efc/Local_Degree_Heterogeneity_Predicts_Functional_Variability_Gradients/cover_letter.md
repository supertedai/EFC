Dear Editors,

We submit "Local Degree Heterogeneity Predicts Functional Variability
Gradients in the Human Connectome" (DOI: 10.6084/m9.figshare.31940370)
for consideration as a Research Article in Network Neuroscience.

Hub regions in the brain display higher functional variability than
peripheral regions, but the structural determinant of this gradient has
not been isolated. We introduce a single scalar measure — the centrifugal
entropy score κ — and show across two independent datasets that:

1. The gradient is robust: κ > 1 across six parcellation atlases (68–400
   regions) and all 30 threshold combinations tested.

2. The gradient is driven by local degree heterogeneity, not global
   algebraic connectivity. In eight individual connectomes, the hub-to-
   periphery degree ratio explains 94% of inter-subject κ variance
   (r = −0.97, p < 0.001), while the Fiedler eigenvalue — the standard
   measure of global connectivity — is unrelated (r = 0.16, p = 0.70).

This dissociation between local and global structural predictors of
functional organisation is, to our knowledge, novel. It has direct
implications for graph-spectral models of brain dynamics and suggests
that κ may serve as a clinically relevant biomarker in conditions
characterised by altered hub connectivity.

All data are publicly available (ENIGMA Toolbox, BCTpy) and analysis
