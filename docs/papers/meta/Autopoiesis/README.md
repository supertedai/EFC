# Autopoiesis

> *Autopoiesis* — from Greek αὐτο (auto, "self") + ποίησις (poiesis,
> "production") — is the meta-principle that **a living system is a
> network of processes that continually produces the very components
> that produce the network, while specifying its own boundary.** Life is
> not a thing; it is an *operational closure* that keeps itself in
> existence by its own metabolism.

**Originators:** Humberto **Maturana** & Francisco **Varela**
(Universidad de Chile), 1972 manuscript → published in English as
*Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*
(Reidel, 1980). Extended into cognitive science by Varela, Thompson &
Rosch, *The Embodied Mind* (1991).

**Modern revival:** Bich, Mossio, Moreno, Bourgine, Ruiz-Mirazo,
Di Paolo (2007–present) — making autopoiesis tractable in
chemistry, computational biology, and AI.

This meta-layer reference brings autopoiesis into the EFC framework as
the **operational-closure** complement to *Homo Fluxus* (the
thermodynamic-flow complement) — together they describe the same
phenomenon from two angles.

## 1. The six criteria of an autopoietic system

Varela (1979) lists six necessary-and-sufficient conditions for a
system to be classified as autopoietic. A network must:

1. Have **identifiable boundaries**.
2. Have **constituent components** producing those boundaries and each
   other.
3. Be a **mechanistic system** — its component properties determine
   its dynamics.
4. The boundaries' components are **themselves produced by the
   network's internal interactions**, not by external assembly.
5. The components of the network are **also produced** by the network's
   internal interactions.
6. All other operations characteristic of the system are subordinate to
   conditions (1)–(5) — i.e., the system is *operationally closed*.

A unit satisfying all six is an *autopoietic system*; the simplest
worked example is a single biological cell. Multicellular organisms,
ecosystems, social systems, and minds are extensions debated under
**second- and third-order autopoiesis**.

## 2. Operational closure vs. thermodynamic openness

A critical (and often misunderstood) point: autopoietic systems are
**operationally closed** but **thermodynamically open**. They:

- *Close* under their own processes — every component is produced by
  the network.
- *Open* on matter and energy — they import low-entropy resources and
  export high-entropy waste.

This is the natural complement to *Homo Fluxus*: the EFC reflection
coefficient `R` measures the *thermodynamic* depth of the closure;
autopoiesis is the *organisational* expression of the same closure.
A system with `R > R_c` *and* satisfying Varela's six criteria is
both a flow subject and a life.

## 3. Cognition without symbols: enaction

In *The Embodied Mind* (Varela, Thompson, Rosch 1991), autopoiesis is
extended into **enactivism**: cognition is not the manipulation of
internal symbols representing an external world but the *enaction* of
a world by an embodied autopoietic agent. The four E-cognition pillars
— **Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, Extended** — all descend from this
move.

| Concept | Autopoietic gloss |
|---|---|
| Perception | Sensorimotor coupling that maintains the network |
| Action | Boundary modulation; reaching that preserves closure |
| Meaning | What an event does to the network's continued existence |
| Self | The operational closure itself |
| World | The *Umwelt* enacted by that closure |

This places autopoiesis as the **constitutive theory** that *FEP* (`../../cognitive/FEP/`),
*PP* (`../../cognitive/PP/`), and *AST* (`../../cognitive/AST/`) all
quietly assume. Free energy minimisation, for example, requires a
self/world boundary in the first place — that boundary is autopoietic.

## 4. Six contested claims about autopoiesis

| # | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| AP1 | Life ≡ autopoiesis (necessary *and* sufficient). | Maturana–Varela position; contested. Bich & Mossio (2017) argue autopoiesis is necessary but adds *organisational closure* + *thermodynamic specification*. |
| AP2 | Cognition is co-extensive with life (every living thing cognises). | Strong Maturana claim; defended by Stewart 1996, Thompson 2007; criticised as collapsing the cognition concept. |
| AP3 | Mind is constituted by autopoietic embodiment (no embodiment, no mind). | Enactivist program (Varela, Thompson, Di Paolo). Contested by computationalists who allow disembodied minds. |
| AP4 | Society is a *third-order* autopoietic system. | Luhmann 1984 — applied to communication systems. Contested for ontological reach. |
| AP5 | Artificial life can be autopoietic in principle. | Bourgine & Stewart 2004 "Autopoiesis and cognition" — affirmative for soft-ALife; sceptical for symbolic AI. |
| AP6 | Autopoiesis grounds a non-reductive yet naturalistic theory of agency. | Di Paolo 2005 "Autopoiesis, adaptivity, teleology, agency"; widely cited in enactivism. |

## 5. Empirical and computational programs

- **Chemoton / minimal autopoietic models** — Gánti's chemoton (1971),
  Luisi's vesicle replicators (lipid micelles that produce their own
  surfactants; Luisi 2003).
- **Computational autopoiesis** — McMullin's "computational autopoiesis"
  re-implementations of Varela's 1974 simulation; Beer's *protocell*
  agents (2014).
- **Bio-cognition continuum** — Levin's bioelectric morphogenesis
  (2019–) is read by enactivists as showing pre-neural autopoietic
  cognition; sceptics call it metaphor.
- **Embodied AI / robotics** — Di Paolo, Rohde, Iizuka (2008) argue
  that artificial autopoiesis is achievable but requires sensorimotor
  closure, not just I/O.

## 7. Criticisms and open issues

1. **Vacuity / inclusivity worry** — Critics (Boden 2000; Bickhard 2009)
   argue autopoiesis is so general it counts hurricanes and flames as
   alive. Defenders (Di Paolo 2005; Bich 2016) add explicit
   *adaptivity* or *agency* requirements.
2. **Cognition-claim overreach** — AP2 (life ≡ cognition) makes
   "cognition" so broad as to be empty for many.
3. **Computational implementability** — McMullin (2004) showed Varela's
   original simulation had bugs; corrected versions sometimes fail to
   re-stabilise. Computational autopoiesis remains hard.
4. **Multicellularity / 2nd-order autopoiesis** — Whether organisms
   are autopoietic *qua organisms* or only their cells is unresolved.
5. **Social autopoiesis (Luhmann)** — Treating communication as the
   "components" of social systems is metaphorical at best for many
   social scientists.
6. **Falsifiability** — As a theoretical framework rather than an
   empirical model, autopoiesis offers categorisations, not directly
   testable predictions. Bich & Mossio (2017) propose operational
   tests; uptake has been slow.
