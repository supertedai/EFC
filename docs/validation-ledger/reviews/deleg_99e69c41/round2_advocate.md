# Round-2 Advocate Rebuttal

## Overall defence

The manuscript's object is a provenance-controlled reconciliation of a model family, not an empirical confirmation paper, a theorem paper, or an independent validation report. Its claims therefore have to be judged against the narrower question it actually answers: whether the corpus forms, status labels, conditional tests, open parameters, and ontological interpretations are represented without inflating them into results. On that standard, round 1 mostly confirms the manuscript's central discipline. The text repeatedly separates inventory from unification, declared derivation from testing, a conditional falsifier from an observed exclusion, an inherited MOND/SPARC scale from an EFC output, and a generic siren ratio from an EFC-specific prediction. Several round-1 findings identify sensible requirements for a future executable analysis; they do not show that the present reconciliation is dishonest. I concede those prospective requirements where appropriate, while rejecting readings that treat an explicitly open selection as a failed validation, treat a candidate regime ontology as an empirical recovery, or impose independent implementation proof on a bounded documentary claim that never made that promise.

## Claim-by-claim defence

### C1 — Three realized response forms occur in the corpus

C1 is an inventory claim: the frozen text displays the three exact forms and maps them to the corpus. It is not a claim that the forms are one dimensionally homogeneous response, one physical object, or three empirically validated implementations.

- **Conceded:** F-form-1 correctly restricts C1 to three model-specific algebraic expressions rather than a common normalized response. F-form-2 correctly observes that the displayed definition is the signed derivative \(\mathrm dS/\mathrm d\rho\), so positivity of a displayed \(\Gamma_i\) does not by itself imply positive entropy production. F-prov-1 correctly notes that DOI-level anchoring is not the same as a version/commit-level readback. F-cart-1, F-alt-1, and F-para-1 correctly warn that corpus occurrence is not paradigm exclusivity or invariant physical distinctness.
- **Defence:** The manuscript itself supplies the limiting premises that the claim-set shorthand omits: model-specific \(S_i\), no common \(\sigma_s\) convention until units are fixed, the residual dimension of \(\Gamma_B\), and the author-gated non-identification of \(\mathrm dS/\mathrm d\rho\), the relativistic source term, and grid-action \(\Gamma\). Those caveats prevent the stronger reading the reviewers reject.
- **Rebuttal:** F-fals-1 imposes a stronger meaning of “realized” than the claim uses by requiring a separately audited executable implementation and supplied tests. The manuscript defines the corpus layering and reports the forms as realized corpus representations; it does not claim that this reconciliation has independently rerun every package. An artifact audit is a valid future verification of realization, but failure to provide that audit does not falsify the documentary inventory. It would do so only if a pinned source were shown not to contain one of the stated forms.

### C2 — The forms are a shape bifurcation, not a demonstrated maturation chain

C2 is deliberately a negative and status-qualified statement. It rejects the unsupported narrative \(\Gamma_A\to\Gamma_B\to\Gamma_C\), while retaining the one declared but undemonstrated B-to-B+ relation.

- **Conceded:** F-prov-2 correctly identifies the lack of row-level/version-level ledger readback. F-cart-2 correctly treats C2 as a compound topology rather than a single empirical proposition. F-alt-2 and F-para-2 correctly state that an internal lineage label has no cross-paradigm or predictive evidential weight. F-fals-2 correctly says that a future matched held-out comparison could establish empirical selection without proving historical genealogy.
- **Defence:** These are precisely the manuscript's qualifications. It says the forms do not constitute a linear maturation chain, calls the structure a shape bifurcation, describes B-to-B+ as declared but undemonstrated, and explicitly denies improved fit, dimensional consistency, or either-direction implication. A later selection would update the open status; it would not show that the present text falsely reported the status at the frozen snapshot.
- **Scope point:** The absence of a current discriminating comparison is not a hidden claim of equivalence. It is the reason the manuscript refuses to call the relation supersession. The reviewers' proposed comparison is therefore a future discriminator, not a counterexample to C2.

### C3 — \(\Gamma_B\) has a residual density dimension and is not commonly normalized

C3 is a formal diagnosis under the manuscript's explicitly declared convention that \(\rho\) and \(\rho_{\rm crit}\) are dimensional densities.

- **Conceded:** F-form-3 and F-alt-3 correctly derive the residual \(\sqrt{[\rho]}\) and correctly distinguish dimensional closure from paradigm selection. F-cart-3 and F-para-3 correctly identify the claim as a compound dimensional, normalization, and provenance statement. F-fals-3 correctly notes that an authoritative prefactor or reference scale could remove the defect. F-prov-3 correctly notes that exact source-version readback is still absent.
- **Defence:** The manuscript says exactly that: \(\Gamma_B\) carries the residual dimension, is not used as a common normalized response until a source-supplied prefactor or reference scale is specified, and the inconsistency is reported rather than silently repaired. The possibility of a future prefactor is not an objection to C3; it is the condition written into C3. Nor does C3 claim that B has been rejected physically. It is a construction-stage warning that blocks an invalid common fit until the units are closed.

### C4 — B-to-B+ is a declared companion upgrade, with supersession unestablished

C4 is a ledger/status claim, not a claim that B+ is physically better.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-4 and F-prov-4 correctly preserve the distinction between a corpus relation label and physical improvement, and F-cart-4 correctly identifies the open-selection phase. F-fals-4 correctly says that a future common-unit comparison could select one form but would not automatically convert the historical label into physical supersession.
- **Defence:** The manuscript expressly says that “upgrade” is ledger vocabulary, not a claim of physical improvement, and separately records supersession as “not established.” Thus the reviewers' strongest warning is already the manuscript's own warning. A versioned ledger readback would strengthen provenance, but no round-1 finding shows that the stated status is misreported.

### C5 — The E-exponent and \(\Gamma\)-shape axes are separate descriptive axes, both open

C5 concerns organization of the EFC model family, not a proof that the axes are physically independent or a claim that the \(\Gamma\)-axis has already been validated.

- **Conceded:** F-form-4 correctly calls the separation descriptive bookkeeping rather than a derived factorization. F-cart-5 and F-para-4 correctly emphasize that “separate” does not mean physically independent. F-alt-5 correctly notes that these are not a complete basis for selecting among all alternative paradigms. F-prov-5 correctly notes that the axis synthesis is not pinned to a row-level/version-level ledger identifier. F-fals-5 correctly observes that the \(\Gamma\)-shape axis has no preregistered discriminator and would need one before a later selection could be treated as a clean validation result.
- **Defence:** Every one of those qualifications appears in the manuscript: the axes are called “descriptive,” physical independence is expressly “not demonstrated,” both statuses are “open,” and only the E-axis has a current preregistration proposal. The absence of a \(\Gamma\)-shape discriminator is not concealed; it is stated in Section 6. F-fals-5 becomes an overreach only if it turns that disclosed open item into a defect in C5. C5 does not claim that the axis is empirically selected or that the two axes span the full theory space.

### C6 — The two square roots play distinct representational roles

C6 is an anti-conflation rule, not an independence theorem. One square root is the exponent in \(E\propto\sqrt g\); the other is the density dependence in \(\Gamma_C\).

- **Conceded:** F-form-5 correctly types the two operations and notes that a shared exponent does not provide a unit or algebraic bridge. F-cart-6 correctly treats C6 as a representational distinction with a non-entailment rule. F-alt-6 correctly notes that square-root scaling is not an EFC signature. F-fals-6 and F-para-5 correctly warn that syntactic distinction does not establish physical independence under all field or normalization redefinitions. F-prov-6 correctly identifies the absence of independent versioned ledger anchoring.
- **Defence:** The manuscript says all of this in the same passage: the roles are distinct, selecting the E-axis exponent does not select the \(\Gamma\)-axis form, and physical independence is not demonstrated. The round-1 concern is valid against a stronger claim, but C6 does not make that stronger claim. No physical mechanism, cross-paradigm uniqueness, or decoupling is inferred merely from the shared notation.

### C7 — KC1 is a conditional falsification criterion

C7 must be read literally as a conditional. It says that if specified robust, bias-controlled data prefer the linear-in-\(g\) exponent form and exclude the square-root form at the stated threshold, then the \(E\propto\sqrt g\) theorem is falsified. It does not say that the antecedent has occurred.

- **Conceded:** F-cart-7 correctly identifies the conjunctive antecedent and the future-testing phase. F-form-6 correctly notes that a fully executable version should make the units of \(a_0\), \(\mu\), and the competing square-root screening expression explicit. F-fals-7 and F-mp-1 correctly observe that “robust,” “bias-controlled,” “preferred,” “diverse,” the pooling rule, the statistic, and the domain still need operational locks. F-para-6 correctly warns that the RAR/SPARC comparator and inferred \(g\) inherit measurement and nuisance choices. F-prov-7 correctly identifies the repository-versus-published-Figshare wording divergence. F-alt-7 correctly limits any resulting falsification to the specified EFC mapping rather than to MOND, \(\Lambda\)CDM, or every screened EFT.
- **Defence:** The manuscript already separates three levels: the conditional wording, the package's internal operator-selection direction, and the unresolved provenance of the nominal \(\ge5\sigma\) statement. It says the threshold is “not a reported result,” records null \(\Delta\chi^2\) and significance, and labels the matter open and author-gated. Therefore the operational gaps identified by round 1 are future preregistration requirements, not evidence that C7 silently reports a fit. The internal package word “observed” is reported as source-package language; it is not promoted to independent observation by the reconciliation.
- **Boundary:** A conditional rule can be logically explicit without being a complete blinded analysis protocol. The manuscript claims the former and explicitly withholds the latter. Requiring the latter to make the former honest would be a scope error.

### C8 — KC1's \(\ge5\sigma\) provenance remains open

C8 is one of the strongest honesty claims in the manuscript: null metadata and “no new fit performed” are treated as a provenance gap, not as a null result and not as confirmation of the threshold.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-8, F-fals-8, F-mp-2, F-para-7, F-prov-8, and F-rep-6 all correctly state that the sigma label cannot be audited or interpreted without the underlying data, likelihood, covariance, nuisance treatment, and source artifact. F-cart-8 correctly identifies C8 as a provenance map rather than a determination that the exclusion is valid.
- **Defence:** The manuscript makes the same distinction explicitly: “no new fit performed,” null fit fields, unresolved whether the statement is an executed fit or an external reference, and an author gate on provenance. None of these findings supplies evidence that the claimed fit exists or that it is false; they reinforce the manuscript's refusal to upgrade it. C8 would be contradicted only if the manuscript presented the nominal exclusion as a reproduced result, which it does not.

### C9 — The SPARC/RAR retrodiction is proposed, unexecuted, and awaits an \(a_0\) freeze

C9 is not a claim that the RAR has validated EFC. It is a proposal-status claim with an anti-shopping condition. The manuscript explicitly says that \(1.2\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) is a provisional source-package value, not an EFC-derived prediction, and that the origin must be labelled MOND/SPARC input, EFC output, or nuisance calibration.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-9 correctly notes that RAR is a shared observable and that agreement would not by itself establish EFC. F-cart-9 correctly preserves the proposal, non-execution, candidate list, and author-freeze requirement. F-fals-9 correctly says that freezing \(a_0\) alone does not freeze the entire nuisance and measurement path. F-form-7 correctly identifies the minor requirement that \(C\) be dimensionless, or otherwise typed, in \(a_{0,\rm eff}=C^2a_0\). F-mp-3 and F-mp-4 correctly identify the raw-signal-to-RAR proxy chain and the calibration ancestry of \(a_0\). F-mp-5 correctly notes that the reconciliation does not itself display the full field-to-acceleration forward operator. F-para-8 correctly treats the inherited MOND/SPARC scale as external calibration unless separately derived. F-prov-9 and F-rep-7 correctly note that the internal candidate source and replay configuration are not yet version-pinned.
- **Defence:** These findings explain why C9 is marked proposed and invalid until exactly one value is author-frozen; they do not refute C9. The manuscript does not pretend that a frozen number becomes independent merely by being frozen, nor that a proxy relation is a raw observable. It calls the retrodiction a discriminator for the E-exponent axis within the proposed EFC test design, not an executed, EFC-unique empirical victory over every alternative paradigm. The future forward map and matched nuisance treatment are prerequisites for execution, and their absence is already reflected in the status “proposed/not executed.”

### C10 — The siren test is proposed, unexecuted, and not EFC-specific until its prediction is frozen

C10 combines a source expression with an explicit limitation on what that expression can presently establish. The ratio \(d_L^{\rm GW}/d_L^{\rm EM}\) is a candidate propagation observable, not a reported anomaly or an EFC-specific result.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-10, F-fals-10, F-mp-7, and F-prov-10 correctly preserve the non-specificity, unexecuted status, and need for a frozen coupling and measurement model. F-form-8 correctly confirms the dimensional consistency of \(F(\phi)=1+\alpha\phi\) under \([\alpha\phi]=1\). F-form-9 correctly refines the mathematical condition: the real positive branch requires positive endpoint factors, and the invariant ratio (or the relevant \(\alpha\phi\) products) is what must be fixed; separately freezing every field normalization parameter is sufficient but not mathematically necessary. F-cart-10 and F-rep-8 correctly identify the proposal's unfrozen parameters and absent replay. F-mp-6 correctly identifies the detector-to-distance proxy chain. F-para-9 and F-para-10 correctly request the background, approximation, calibration, systematic, and alternative-propagation conditions needed for an actual inference.
- **Defence:** The manuscript's “only when frozen” wording is a conservative identifiability gate, not a claim that each raw parameter is separately observable or that freezing alone proves EFC. F-form-9 is a useful refinement of what “frozen” can mean; it does not change the status. The displayed formula does not imply inequality, and the manuscript does not say that it does. It proposes a future test of whether a non-unit ratio occurs, states that the ratio is not EFC-specific until the coupling is fixed, and leaves the parameters author-gated. The round-1 measurement and paradigm concerns are therefore requirements for a future executed discriminator, not a contradiction of C10.

### C11 — No new fit, axis selection, or independent package validation is claimed here

C11 is explicitly bounded by “in this work.” It is not a universal claim that no fit exists anywhere in the corpus, and it is not a claim that inherited source-package numbers have been independently reproduced.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-11 correctly says that this scope statement cannot favor EFC over alternatives. F-cart-11 correctly identifies the three separate negative scope statements. F-prov-11 and F-rep-1 correctly observe that inherited numerical results remain inherited and that no new execution evidence is claimed.
- **Defence:** F-fals-11's proposed repository audit is a sensible way to verify execution history, but its stronger implication is not accepted. The manuscript does not ask the reader to infer a universal absence of external work from silence. It says the reconciliation itself performed no new fit, made no named axis selection, and claims no independent validation; the “What is not tested” section enumerates those boundaries. Internal reviewer archives are explicitly called reading consistency, not independent validation. An audit may verify the bounded self-report, but the absence of an audit does not turn C11 into a claim of completed validation.

### C12 — Byte-for-byte reproducibility is open; the reported current build is warning-free

C12 contains two deliberately separate workflow propositions. The first says byte identity has not been achieved because of known pdfTeX metadata; the second reports the warning status of the current build. It does not equate a clean build with byte reproducibility or with scientific validation.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-12 and F-rep-2 correctly state that the manuscript keeps document-build reproducibility separate from physical validation and separate from byte identity. F-cart-12 and F-fals-12 correctly split the byte-level and warning-level tests. F-prov-12 correctly notes that the snapshot does not carry a commit, build log, or checksum sidecar. F-para-11 correctly says that build warnings are engineering proxies, not evidence for a physical model. F-rep-3 and F-rep-4 correctly identify the need to retain logs, pin the toolchain, and independently verify the warning-free subclaim. F-rep-5 correctly notes that the full AI-friendly package stack is described prospectively and that a current package inventory would need a manifest check.
- **Defence:** The text does not claim achieved byte reproducibility; it says the opposite and gives the stated reason. Nor does it present the warning-free sentence as an independently archived measurement. The “designed to carry” language is prospective, not a claim that the current directory already contains every future `src/`, `data/`, and `examples/` subtree. Thus the reproducer's artifact and packaging concerns are valid workflow follow-up, but they do not falsify C12 or expose an internal contradiction.

### C13 — MOND-like and \(\Lambda\)CDM-like behaviour are candidate regimes, not established unification

C13 is the claim most vulnerable to inflation if its qualifiers are stripped away, which is why the manuscript states them repeatedly. The formal-limit rows and the ontological regime reading are separate. The latter is explicitly described as a hypothesis, “not an established result.”

- **Conceded:** F-form-10 correctly says that no typed limiting map is supplied in this reconciliation and that C13 cannot be promoted to a mathematical equivalence. F-cart-13 correctly identifies the three layers: candidate ontology, formal-limit declarations, and regime interpretation. F-prov-13 correctly notes that the broader package identifiers lack the same full DOI/version presentation as Table 1. F-alt-13 correctly says that generic effective-regime syntax is not unique to EFC. F-fals-13 correctly says that the regime reading is not yet an operational falsifiable classifier. F-mp-8 and F-mp-9 correctly note the absent pre-data regime boundaries, target observable, and equal-footing holdout. F-para-12 and F-para-13 correctly request typed dimensionless control variables, order-of-limits information, and provenance for any proxy coordinates.
- **Defence:** These are all valid requirements for a future empirical or formal promotion of the ontology. They are not a rebuttal to the claim as written. The manuscript distinguishes a declared formal limit from an ontological interpretation, says neither row is an equal-footing empirical recovery, and states that the regime reading remains a hypothesis. It does not claim that the names MOND and \(\Lambda\)CDM are themselves observables, that the limits commute, that the parent action has been completed, or that the two behaviours have been jointly validated. Calling C13 “not operationally falsifiable yet” is therefore a correct description of its current status, not a discovery that the manuscript misrepresented it.
- **Provenance point:** The bare broader identifiers are a documentation weakness and should be expanded in a later package, but C13 does not depend on pretending that those identifiers constitute independent evidence. The manuscript explicitly calls the cited packages source-corpus material and the review archive internal reading consistency.

### C14 — The provenance scope is non-exclusive: six primary packages plus broader cited corpus packages

C14 is a scope claim, not a claim that all cited packages share one implementation, that the corpus is complete, or that package breadth validates EFC.

- **Conceded:** F-alt-14 correctly says that corpus breadth is not cross-paradigm evidence. F-cart-14 correctly identifies the non-exclusive topology. F-fals-14 correctly proposes a registry audit of the inclusion boundary. F-para-14 correctly notes that persistent identifiers alone do not close every transitive semantic dependency. F-prov-14 and F-rep-9 correctly identify the missing full DOI/version/commit/checksum detail for the broader identifiers.
- **Defence:** The manuscript explicitly lists six primary packages in Table 1 and then separately names broader packages in the review-status section and provenance note. It says the six are not an exclusive closed set and says package metadata are heterogeneous and not ground truth. Nothing in C14 claims physical unity, completeness, independent validation, or a closed executable measurement chain. The round-1 concerns justify better identifier-level documentation; they do not overturn the scope statement that is visibly made.
